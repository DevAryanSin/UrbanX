1. PROJECT VISION & MOTIVATION
================================================================================

UrbanX is an AI-powered urban redesign system that analyzes existing Indian city
infrastructure and generates pedestrian-friendly redesigns meeting international
standards. The goal is to transform Indian cities from car-dominated, pedestrian-
hostile environments into walkable, safe, accessible, and green urban spaces —
using the standards of well-developed nations (Copenhagen, Amsterdam, Tokyo,
Barcelona, Singapore) as benchmarks.

The system is purpose-built for Indian urban conditions: monsoon flooding,
encroached sidewalks, mixed traffic (auto-rickshaws, cycle-rickshaws, cows),
open drains, zero accessibility, extreme heat, and the world's highest pedestrian
death toll (~15,000/year).

Constraints:
- $0 budget — all tools, APIs, models, and hosting are free
- Solo developer — no ML/ML team
- 3-month timeline to complete MVP
- No training of ML models — pretrained/open-source only
- Output must be directly usable by civil/road engineers

3. THE VISION — WHAT "PEDESTRIAN-FRIENDLY" ACTUALLY MEANS
================================================================================

BEFORE (Typical Indian Street):
- 6-lane road, speeding vehicles
- No median, no separation
- Broken/absent sidewalks (encroached by vendors, parked vehicles)
- Open drains on both sides
- No pedestrian crossings for 400m+
- No trees, no shade
- No accessibility features
- No pedestrian-scale lighting

AFTER (Pedestrian-Friendly Redesign):
- Road diet: 6 lanes → 2 vehicle lanes
- 2.5m continuous sidewalk on both sides (smooth, shaded)
- 1.5m protected bike lane (green paint)
- 3.0m vehicle lanes (narrower = slower speeds)
- Raised zebra crossings every 80-100m with refuge islands
- Pedestrian signals with countdown timers
- Street trees every 8m (native species: Neem, Peepal, Ashoka)
- Covered drains + rain gardens/bioswales
- Tactile paving at all crossings
- Curb ramps at every crossing
- Pedestrian-scale LED lighting every 15m
- Bollards to prevent vehicles mounting sidewalks

5. INPUT METHODS
================================================================================

Three ways for users to specify the area to analyze:

A) PHOTO UPLOAD (Street-Level)
   - User uploads a photo taken from their phone
   - Vision pipeline analyzes the image for infrastructure conditions
   - GPS EXIF data (if available) used to geolocate
   - If no GPS, user confirms location on map

B) COORDINATES
   - User enters latitude/longitude or draws a polygon
   - System fetches OSM data, Street View, satellite imagery
   - Analysis covers the defined area

C) GOOGLE MAPS INTERACTION
   - Interactive map in the web app
   - User clicks to select a point or draws a boundary
   - System auto-fetches all data for the selected area

   - Address search, reverse geocoding
   - URL: https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/

7. VISION ANALYSIS PIPELINE
================================================================================

Three pretrained models — zero training required:

A) YOLOv9 (Object Detection)
   - Model: yolov9c.pt (pretrained, free from Ultralytics)
   - Detects: vehicles, pedestrians, encroachments, road defects,
     sidewalk conditions, street furniture, infrastructure elements
   - India-specific classes (see Section 8)

B) LLaVA 1.6 (Scene Understanding)
   - Model: llava-hf/llava-v1.6-mistral-7b-hf (free, HuggingFace)
   - Runs on Google Colab T4 GPU (free)
   - Returns JSON with: building_type, sidewalk_present, crosswalk_type,
     road_condition, land_use, problems_identified, encroachment_type

C) EasyOCR (Sign Reading)
   - Free, open-source OCR
   - Reads: speed limit signs, street name boards, Hindi text,
     directional signs, warning signs

9. ANALYSIS ENGINE — SCORING & AUDITS
================================================================================

The system performs 7 types of analysis:

1. PEDESTRIAN INFRASTRUCTURE AUDIT
   - Sidewalk coverage (% of road length with sidewalk)
   - Sidewalk condition score (1-5 scale)
   - Encroachment inventory (vendors, parking, construction, utilities)
   - Crossing spacing (average distance between crossings)
   - Crossing type inventory (none/zebra/signalized/grade-separated)
   - Accessibility score (ramps, tactile paving, audible signals)
   - Comparison to IRC:SP:103 (Indian pedestrian guidelines)

2. PEDESTRIAN SAFETY ANALYSIS
   - Pedestrian conflict points (vehicle-pedestrian crossing angles)
   - Speed analysis (85th percentile speed vs. design speed)
   - Crash risk score (based on speed, volume, crossing distance)
   - School zone safety audit
   - Night safety audit (lighting assessment)
   - Comparison to Vision Zero standards

3. WALKABILITY SCORE
   - Walk Score (0-100): Based on sidewalk quality, crossing frequency,
     block size, land use mix, shade coverage
   - 800m walkability: % of residents within 800m of daily needs
     (grocery, school, transit, park, healthcare)
   - Comfort index: Shade coverage + surface quality + obstruction-free
   - Safety index: Crossing safety + lighting + traffic speed
   - Comparison to benchmark cities

4. MONSOON RESILIENCE SCORE
   - Drainage coverage (% of road with covered drains)
   - Flooding risk (low points, inadequate drainage)
   - Permeable surface ratio
   - Rain garden / bioretention coverage
   - Monsoon walkability (can you walk during rain?)

5. ACCESSIBILITY AUDIT
   - Curb ramp coverage (% of crossings with ramps)
   - Tactile paving coverage
   - Audible signal coverage
   - Wheelchair route continuity
   - Compliance with Harmonised Guidelines (India's accessibility standard)

6. GREEN COVERAGE
   - Tree canopy coverage (% of road length)
   - Green space per capita (m²/person)
   - Park access (% of residents within 400m of a park)
   - Heat island mitigation score
   - Comparison to WHO standard (9m² green space per person)

7. RECOMMENDATIONS (Prioritized)
   CRITICAL (immediate safety):
   - Install crossings at specific locations
   - Reduce speed to 30 km/h on specific roads
   - Cover open drains at specific locations
   - Install pedestrian signals at specific intersections

   HIGH (within 6 months):
   - Road diet on specific road from 4 lanes to 2
   - Add 2.0m sidewalk on both sides of specific road
   - Plant street trees every 8m on specific road
   - Install pedestrian-scale lighting on specific road

   MEDIUM (within 1 year):
   - Add bike lanes on specific roads
   - Create rain gardens in medians
   - Designate hawker zones off sidewalk
   - Build footbridge at specific highway crossing

   LONG-TERM (within 5 years):
   - Implement superblock pattern in specific area
   - Create car-free zone in specific commercial area
   - Build underground pedestrian network at transit hub
   - Implement BRT on specific corridor

10. INDIA-SPECIFIC DESIGN RULES (HARD CONSTRAINTS)
================================================================================

These rules are STRUCTURALLY BAKED IN — the redesign engine cannot produce
a design that violates any rule. They are deterministic constraints, not prompts.

RULE 1: CONTINUOUS SIDEWALK NETWORK
- Every road segment has sidewalks on BOTH sides
- Minimum width: 2.0m (2.5m preferred, 3.0m in commercial areas)
- Surface: Smooth, even, non-slip
- Obstruction-free: No poles, signs, vendors, or parked vehicles on sidewalk
- Tactile paving at every crossing (warning blocks + directional blocks)
- Curb ramps at every crossing (1.5m wide, 1:12 slope max)
- Sidewalk slope 1-2% away from road (monsoon drainage)
- Tree pits integrated into sidewalk (not blocking walking path)

RULE 2: FREQUENT, SAFE CROSSINGS
- Pedestrian crossing every 80-150m on all roads
- Types (priority order):
  1. Raised zebra crossing + refuge island (local roads)
  2. Signalized crossing + countdown timer (collector/arterial)
  3. Grade-separated crossing (highways, 4+ lanes, speed > 50 km/h)
- Refuge islands are CRITICAL (roads too wide to cross in one phase)
- Pedestrian signal timing: 1.2 m/s walk speed (slower than Western 1.5 m/s
  because of elderly, children, disabled, and general unfamiliarity)

RULE 3: ROAD DIET (REDUCE VEHICLE DOMINANCE)
- Maximum 2 vehicle lanes per direction on urban roads
- Lane width: 3.0m (not 3.5m — narrower = slower speeds)
- Remove excess lanes → convert to:
  - Bike lanes (1.5m)
  - Wider sidewalks (add 1.0-1.5m)
  - Median with trees (1.5-2.0m)
  - On-street parking (2.5m)
  - Rain garden / bioswale (1.0-1.5m)
- Must maintain emergency vehicle access (fire truck needs 3.5m clear)

RULE 4: STREET TREE CANOPY
- 80% of road length has continuous tree canopy
- Spacing: Every 8-10m
- Species: Native, shade-providing, drought-resistant
  (Neem, Peepal, Banyan, Ashoka, Gulmohar, Rain Tree)
- Tree pit: 1.5m x 1.5m minimum, permeable surface
- Root management: Root barrier to protect sidewalk
- Indian context: Summer temperatures exceed 45°C — shade is survival

RULE 5: MONSOON-RESILIENT DRAINAGE
- Zero pedestrian flooding during normal rainfall
- Covered drains along all roads (no open drains)
- Permeable surfaces for sidewalks and parking areas
- Rain gardens / bioswales in medians and buffer zones
- Minimum drainage gradient: 0.5%
- Curb inlets every 15-20m connecting to storm sewer
- Design for intense rainfall (50-100mm/hour events)

RULE 6: ZERO PEDESTRIAN DEATHS (VISION ZERO)
- Speed management:
  - Local roads: 30 km/h (with speed humps)
  - Collector roads: 40 km/h (with raised crossings)
  - Arterial roads: 50 km/h (with signalized crossings + refuge islands)
  - School zones: 20 km/h (with flashing beacons + crossing guard)
- Physical barrier between vehicles and pedestrians on roads > 40 km/h
- Bollards at crossing points (prevent vehicles mounting sidewalk)
- Guard rails at high-speed locations
- Minimum 10 lux on sidewalks (pedestrian-scale LED lighting)
- Reducing speed from 50 to 30 km/h reduces pedestrian death risk from 85% to 5%

11. REDESIGN ENGINE — CONSTRAINT-BASED GENERATIVE DESIGN
================================================================================

WHY NOT GAN/GNN/RLHF?
- User has no AI/ML background
- Training is expensive and unstable
- Hard constraints guarantee rule compliance — a GAN cannot
- Deterministic algorithms are predictable, debuggable, and free

APPROACH: Constraint-based deterministic engine with these algorithms:
- Road insertion algorithm
- Land use rezoning algorithm
- Green space placement algorithm
- Street width reduction algorithm
- Crossing insertion algorithm
- Sidewalk generation algorithm
- Tree placement algorithm
- Drainage network design algorithm
- Speed management device placement algorithm

Each algorithm enforces the hard constraints from Section 10.
The engine validates every output against all 6 rule categories before producing it.

13. OUTPUT FORMATS
================================================================================

The system produces 8 output formats:

1. GeoJSON
   - GIS import (QGIS, ArcGIS)
   - Feature properties include: feature_type, road_class, lanes,
     lane_width_m, speed_limit_kph, surface, sidewalk_left, sidewalk_right,
     sidewalk_width_m, bike_lane, bike_lane_width_m, parking, median_type,

   - Civil engineering standard for road design software

3. SUMO Traffic Simulation Files
   - .net.xml — road network definition
   - .rou.xml — vehicle and pedestrian routes
   - .poly.xml — polygon features (buildings, parks)
   - SUMO = Simulation of Urban MObility (free, open-source)

4. DWG (AutoCAD)
   - 2D/3D CAD drawing
   - Compatible with: AutoCAD, BricsCAD, DraftSight, LibreCAD
   - Layers: roads, sidewalks, bike lanes, trees, drainage, crossings

5. PDF Report
   - India-focused analysis report
   - All 7 audit sections (see Section 9)
   - Before/After visualizations
   - Prioritized recommendations
   - IRC compliance checks
   - Comparison to benchmark cities

6. BOQ + Cost Estimate (CSV/Excel)
   - Bill of Quantities with Indian schedule of rates
   - Item-wise cost breakdown
   - Total project cost estimate
   - Per-meter costs for sidewalk, drainage, trees, etc.

7. Before/After Images
   - Visual comparison of existing vs. redesigned street
   - Rendered from the redesign data

8. Interactive Leaflet Map
   - Web-based interactive map
   - Toggle layers: existing, proposed, analysis overlays
   - Click any feature for details

15. BENCHMARK CITIES
================================================================================

The system compares Indian city conditions to these benchmarks:

COPENHAGEN, DENMARK (Cycling + Walking):
- 62% of residents cycle to work/school
- 3.5m wide cycle tracks (separated from road)
- Green wave for cyclists
- Car-free zones in city center
- Pedestrianized Stroget (longest pedestrian street in Europe)
- Target: Connaught Place, Delhi / MG Road, Bangalore

AMSTERDAM, NETHERLANDS (Complete Streets):
- Woonerf (shared space) design
- 400km of dedicated cycle paths
- Traffic calming on all residential streets
- Pedestrians have absolute priority in shared zones
- Target: Residential neighborhoods in Indian cities

TOKYO, JAPAN (Transit + Pedestrian Integration):
- Pedestrian flow analysis at every station
- Underground pedestrian networks connecting stations
- Escalators and elevators at every crossing
- Tactile paving everywhere (since 1965!)
- Zero pedestrian deaths in many wards (Vision Zero)

19. API CACHING STRATEGY
================================================================================

All API responses are cached in Redis to stay within free limits:

class APICache:
    def get_or_fetch(self, prefix, params, fetch_fn, ttl_seconds=86400):
        key = self._make_key(prefix, params)
        cached = self.redis.get(key)
        if cached:
            return json.loads(cached)
        result = fetch_fn()
        self.redis.setex(key, ttl_seconds, json.dumps(result))
        return result

Cache TTLs:
- OSM data: 7 days (urban features don't change often)
- Street View images: 30 days
- Satellite imagery: 30 days
- OSRM routes: 7 days
- Google Places: 7 days
- Vision analysis results: 30 days

23. STAR METHOD RESUME POINTS
================================================================================

SITUATION:
Indian cities are among the most dangerous in the world for pedestrians, with
~15,000 pedestrian deaths annually (40% of all road fatalities). Most urban
roads lack sidewalks, marked crossings, accessibility features, and shade —
while being dominated by 4-6 lane car-centric designs with speeding vehicles,
open drains, and zero pedestrian priority. Existing urban planning tools are
either too generic (not India-specific) or require expensive proprietary
software and ML expertise that solo developers and small municipal bodies
don't have.

TASK:
Build a complete, free-to-use system that takes minimal input (a photo,
coordinates, or Google Maps selection), analyzes the existing pedestrian
infrastructure using AI vision, scores it against international pedestrian-
friendliness standards and Indian road codes (IRC:SP:103, IRC:86, NBC 2016),
and generates a fully redesigned street layout — outputting production-ready
files that civil engineers and city planners can directly use for implementation.

ACTION:
Designed and developed a multi-modal analysis pipeline combining YOLOv9 for
India-specific object detection (encroachments, potholes, auto-rickshaws,
open drains, sidewalk defects), LLaVA 1.6 for scene-level understanding
(land use, street character, infrastructure assessment), and EasyOCR for sign
reading (speed limits, street names in Hindi/English). Built a deterministic
constraint-based redesign engine (replacing GAN/GNN approaches) that enforces
6 hard rule categories: continuous sidewalk network (>=2.5m, tactile paving,
curb ramps), safe crossing network (every 80-150m, raised zebra + refuge
islands + countdown signals), road diet algorithm (4-6 lanes -> 2 lanes +
bike lane + wider sidewalk + median), street tree canopy (every 8m, native
species like Neem/Peepal/Ashoka), monsoon-resilient covered drainage with
rain gardens, and Vision Zero speed management (30 km/h local, 20 km/h school
zones). Integrated OSM data, Google Maps Platform APIs ($200/mo free tier),
and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery for comprehensive area analysis. Implemented
7 output format renderers: GeoJSON, LandXML (Civil 3D), SUMO simulation files
(.net.xml, .rou.xml, .poly.xml), DWG (AutoCAD), PDF analysis reports with
IRC compliance checks, CSV/Excel BOQ with Indian rates, and interactive Leaflet
before/after maps.

RESULT:
Delivered a fully functional MVP that ingests a single photo or set of
coordinates and produces a complete pedestrian-friendly street redesign with
prioritized implementation recommendations (CRITICAL -> LONG-TERM), cost
estimates, and engineering-grade output files — reducing a process that
traditionally takes weeks of manual survey and design work to under 30 seconds.
The system is built entirely on free resources (open-source models, Google's
free API tier, free hosting), requires zero ML training or expertise to operate,
and is specifically calibrated for Indian urban conditions including monsoon
resilience, encroachment management, mixed traffic, and accessibility compliance.
